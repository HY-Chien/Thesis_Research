This thesis proposes SEEK (Self-certainty Evaluated Evidence-gated Knowledge-retrieval), a lightweight adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for open-domain question answering. Fixed retrieval can distract simple questions and miss intermediate entities needed by multi-hop questions. Adaptive RAG must decide both whether to retrieve and whether decomposition can expose evidence without discarding global context. Existing approaches rely on coarse routing, imperfect uncertainty signals, or costly learned policies.

SEEK couples step-level retrieval gating with late path selection. A compact decomposer receives one LoRA supervised fine-tuning pass on 12,000 examples from MuSiQue, 2WikiMultihopQA, and PopQA. At inference, SEEK retains direct and decomposed candidates. It measures how much token probability falls outside the model's five leading candidates; this confidence score, called Tail Leakage, decides whether each sub-question retrieves evidence. Original-question evidence is then added to the decomposed contexts, and a deterministic, training-free selector---the verifier---chooses the final answer using confidence, decomposition stability, and answer type. Decomposition thus remains reversible and routing inspectable.

SEEK coordinates question decomposition, step-level retrieval, and final path selection with lightweight training. Decomposition exposes intermediate evidence needed for multi-hop reasoning; Tail Leakage distinguishes unreliable answers more effectively than maximum token entropy and provides a controllable accuracy--retrieval trade-off; and the dual-path design preserves the complementary strengths of direct answering and decomposed reasoning. SEEK reaches 41.88 EM and 48.90 F1 on average across four datasets.
