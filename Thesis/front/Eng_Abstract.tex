This study proposes SEEK (Self-certainty Evaluated Entropy-gated Knowledge-retrieval), a lightweight adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for open-domain question answering. Standard RAG systems apply a fixed retrieval strategy to every query, which can inject noise on simple questions and miss intermediate evidence on multi-hop questions. Existing adaptive methods either route at a coarse question level, probe uncertainty at a noisy token level, or learn expensive sub-question policies through tree search and preference optimisation.

SEEK uses a compact KG-triple decomposer trained with a single LoRA SFT pass on a 12K mixture of MuSiQue, 2WikiMultihopQA, and PopQA examples. The final system no longer treats entropy as a single hard skip gate. Instead, it generates both a direct answer and a decomposed answer, preserves original-question evidence inside decomposed sub-question contexts, and applies a training-free verifier with a conservative type-aware fallback. This late-routing design preserves the interpretability of entropy-based methods while avoiding the brittleness of a global threshold.

Under the honest wiki-21M retrieval protocol aligned with DeepRAG, SEEK reaches an average of 40.52 EM and 47.61 F1, exceeding DeepRAG's 38.45 EM and 45.75 F1. SEEK improves over DeepRAG on 2WikiMultihopQA (50.00 EM), MuSiQue (24.70 EM), and PopQA (51.90 EM), while remaining behind on HotpotQA (35.50 EM versus 40.70 EM). Analysis shows that direct and decomposed paths are complementary, that oracle direct-vs-decompose routing still offers 3.8 to 6.1 EM of headroom, and that entropy is useful as a sub-question retrieval signal but not as a correctness guarantee.
