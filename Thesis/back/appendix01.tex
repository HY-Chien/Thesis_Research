\section{Decomposition Failures}
\label{app:decomp-failure-analysis}

We use the final accuracy-first step gate ($\tau=0.002305$) and assign one main cause to each incorrect decomposition answer. Step answers are reselected and resynthesised at this threshold, while the sub-question wording stays fixed. Evidence is covered when the required Wikipedia titles appear in the top 10 results from either the original question or the resolved sub-questions.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption[Decomposition Failures]{Main causes of decomposition errors.}
    \label{tab:failure-taxonomy}
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{ p{0.48\textwidth} *{3}{>{\centering\arraybackslash}p{0.135\textwidth}} }
        \toprule
        Primary cause & \shortstack{HotpotQA\\($N=640$)} & \shortstack{2Wiki\\($N=503$)} & \shortstack{MuSiQue\\($N=733$)} \\
        \midrule
        Missing required evidence & 307 (48.0\%) & 311 (61.8\%) & 513 (70.0\%) \\
        Incorrect step answer with complete evidence & 131 (20.5\%) & 98 (19.5\%) & 73 (10.0\%) \\
        Correct steps but wrong final answer & 164 (25.6\%) & 92 (18.3\%) & 138 (18.8\%) \\
        Lost constraint; original retrieval has complete evidence & 38 (5.9\%) & 2 (0.4\%) & 9 (1.2\%) \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Missing evidence is the largest failure category on all three multi-hop datasets. Other errors mainly come from incorrect step answers or final synthesis. Lost constraints are less common and occur mostly on HotpotQA.

\section{Confidence Signals}
\label{app:confidence-signals}

Table~\ref{tab:confidence-signal-definitions} defines the confidence signals used in Section~\ref{sec:tail-leakage-analysis}. Here, $p_t(v)$ is a next-token probability, $y_t$ is the generated token, and $T$ is the answer length. A higher score means greater uncertainty.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption[Confidence Signal Definitions]{Confidence signal definitions.}
    \label{tab:confidence-signal-definitions}
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{8pt}
    \begin{tabular}{ >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.28\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.62\textwidth} }
        \toprule
        Signal & Definition \\
        \midrule
        Maximum token entropy
        & Largest token-level entropy in the answer: $\max_t[-\sum_v p_t(v)\log p_t(v)]$~\cite{maqa,targ}. \\
        \addlinespace
        Answer likelihood
        & Total negative log-likelihood of the generated answer: $-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p_t(y_t)$~\cite{semanticuncertainty}. \\
        \addlinespace
        TARG margin
        & Average transformed gap between the two largest logits over the first eight tokens~\cite{targ}. \\
        \addlinespace
        Sample disagreement
        & Fraction of ten sampled answers that differ from the greedy answer~\cite{maqa,semanticuncertainty,semanticentropy}. \\
        \addlinespace
        P(True) self-check
        & Model-estimated probability that its answer is false: $1-P(\mathrm{True}\mid q,C,\hat a)$~\cite{kadavath2022}. \\
        \addlinespace
        Tail Leakage
        & Cumulative probability leakage outside the top-five token set: $-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\sum_{v\in V_5(p_t)}p_t(v)$. \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

All signals use the same direct-path outputs and are ranked by AUROC for exact-match errors. Except for sample disagreement and P(True), they come from the same greedy decoding pass.
