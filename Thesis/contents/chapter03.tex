\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:method}

This chapter presents \textbf{SEEK} (\textbf{S}elf-certainty \textbf{E}valuated \textbf{E}ntropy-gated \textbf{K}nowledge-retrieval), a lightweight adaptive RAG framework that treats question decomposition as a routing problem. Earlier versions of SEEK used entropy as a hard skip-decomposition gate. The final system instead runs both a direct path and a decomposition path, preserves original-question evidence inside decomposed steps, and chooses between the candidate answers using a training-free verifier. Section~\ref{sec:overview} gives the end-to-end overview. Sections~\ref{sec:decomposer}--\ref{sec:synthesize} describe the decomposition path, Section~\ref{sec:verifier} describes the verifier, and Sections~\ref{sec:training}--\ref{sec:modes} give the training and implementation details.

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

Given a question $q$ and an open-domain retrieval corpus $\mathcal{P}$, SEEK produces a final answer $\hat{a}$ through two candidate-generation paths followed by a verifier:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Direct path.} The answerer directly answers the original question $q$ with BM25 evidence retrieved from $\mathcal{P}$, producing a direct candidate $a^{\mathrm{dir}}$ and a token-level entropy score $\sigma^{\mathrm{dir}}$ over the generated answer span.
    \item \textbf{Decomposition path.} The decomposer $\mathcal{D}_\theta$ maps $q$ to ordered sub-questions $\{s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$. Each sub-question is answered sequentially, with placeholder substitution, entropy probing, conditional retrieval, original-question evidence union, and a final synthesis step that produces $a^{\mathrm{dec}}$.
    \item \textbf{Training-free verifier.} A deterministic verifier compares $a^{\mathrm{dir}}$ and $a^{\mathrm{dec}}$ using query-level signals: direct-answer confidence, decomposition stability, post-synthesis confidence, and answer agreement. It returns either $a^{\mathrm{dir}}$ or $a^{\mathrm{dec}}$ as $\hat{a}$.
\end{enumerate}

The final operating point is the \textbf{training-free verifier with type-aware fallback}: it uses only signals naturally emitted during inference and does not require an additional classifier, DPO, tree search, or gold-answer supervision. Evidence-support variants are evaluated as ablations in Chapter~\ref{ch:exp}, but are not used in the main method because lexical support is not globally stable for compositional reasoning.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{M}_\theta$ be a pretrained language model, $r(q, \mathcal{P}, k)$ a BM25 retriever over an open-domain corpus $\mathcal{P}$, and $\mathcal{D}_\theta(q)$ a decomposer that emits sub-questions. A conventional retrieve-then-read system produces one answer from one path. SEEK instead constructs two candidates:

\[
    a^{\mathrm{dir}} = \mathcal{M}_\theta(q, r(q, \mathcal{P}, k)),
\]
\[
    a^{\mathrm{dec}} = \mathrm{Synth}\left(q, \{(s_i, a_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}\right), \quad \{s_i\}_{i=1}^{n}=\mathcal{D}_\theta(q).
\]

The final decision is a verifier $v$:

\[
    \hat{a}=v\left(q, a^{\mathrm{dir}}, a^{\mathrm{dec}}, z^{\mathrm{dir}}, z^{\mathrm{dec}}\right),
\]

where $z^{\mathrm{dir}}$ and $z^{\mathrm{dec}}$ are inference-time diagnostic signals such as entropy, unresolved placeholders, and candidate agreement. Unlike DeepRAG~\cite{deeprag}, SEEK does not learn $v$ through tree-search rollouts or preference optimisation. The verifier is a transparent rule set designed to approximate the oracle choice between direct and decomposed reasoning.

\section{Stage 1: Sub-Question Decomposer}
\label{sec:decomposer}

\subsection{Output Format}

The decomposer emits one sub-question per line, prefixed with \texttt{\#}$\langle i \rangle$\texttt{:}. Each sub-question is expressed as a compact knowledge-graph fragment:

\begin{center}
    \texttt{\#}$i$\texttt{: }$\langle$\emph{entity}$\rangle$\texttt{ >> }$\langle$\emph{relation}$\rangle$
\end{center}

When a sub-question depends on an earlier answer, the placeholder \texttt{\#}$j$ refers to step $j$. For example:

\begin{verbatim}
#1: X >> director
#2: #1 >> place of birth
\end{verbatim}

At inference, placeholders are resolved sequentially: once $a_1$ is produced, \texttt{\#1} in $s_2$ is rewritten as $a_1$ before retrieval and answering. The KG-triple notation is compact, consistent across datasets, and aligned with Wikidata-style supervision in 2WikiMultihopQA and PopQA.

\subsection{Decomposer Prompt}

The decomposer is prompted with a fixed instruction:

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Decompose the following question into an ordered list of atomic sub-questions. Each sub-question must be answerable in a single retrieval and reasoning step. When a sub-question depends on the answer of an earlier one, refer to that step with a \texttt{\#N} placeholder. Output one sub-question per line, prefixed with \texttt{\#<index>:}
\end{quote}

\section{Stage 2: Token-Level Confidence Probe}
\label{sec:probe}

Both the direct path and each sub-question answer use an answer-only prompt:

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
Answer the question with the answer phrase only. No explanation, no preamble, no full sentence --- just the answer.
\end{quote}

During greedy decoding, SEEK records the softmax distribution $p_t=\mathrm{softmax}(\mathrm{logits}_t)$ for each generated token $t$ and computes:

\begin{align}
    \sigma^{\text{max-ent}}  &= \max_t H(p_t), \\
    \sigma^{\text{mean-ent}} &= \frac{1}{T}\sum_t H(p_t), \\
    \sigma^{\text{min-lp}}   &= \min_t \log p_t(y_t),
\end{align}

where $H(p)=-\sum_v p(v)\log p(v)$ and EOS tokens are excluded. The default diagnostic signal is maximum token entropy. In the decomposition path, this entropy controls per-step retrieval. In the v2 verifier, it is used at the query level to decide whether the direct answer is confident enough to rescue a failed decomposition.

\section{Stage 3: Decomposition-Path Retrieval Gate}
\label{sec:gate}

Within the decomposition path, retrieval remains sub-question-level. For a resolved sub-question $s_i$, the answerer first produces a tentative parametric answer $\tilde{a}_i$ and entropy $\sigma_i$. The retriever is invoked when:

\[
    \pi(s_i)=\mathbb{1}[\sigma_i \geq \tau],
\]

with $\tau=0.3$ for max-entropy in the final system. If $\pi(s_i)=1$, BM25 retrieves top-$k$ passages and the answerer re-answers $s_i$ with context; otherwise $\tilde{a}_i$ is kept. This local gate reduces unnecessary retrieval inside the decomposition path, but it is no longer the final routing mechanism for the whole question.

\subsection{Original-Question Evidence Union}
\label{sec:direct-context-union}

A decomposed sub-question is often shorter and more entity-centric than the original question, which improves retrieval precision but can remove global clues such as comparison targets or disambiguating conditions. To preserve this context, the final system augments each retrieved sub-question context with evidence retrieved from the original question:

\[
    C_i = r(s_i, \mathcal{P}, k) \cup r(q, \mathcal{P}, k).
\]

The union is deduplicated before answering. This operation does not change the decomposer or train a new retriever; it simply keeps the original-question evidence available when answering each decomposed step. Chapter~\ref{ch:exp} shows that this is the main improvement from the path-aware baseline to the final system, especially on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA.


\subsection{Retriever}

The final experiments use an honest open-domain Wikipedia corpus: the shared Elasticsearch \texttt{wiki} index over \texttt{psgs\_w100} with approximately 21M passages, matching DeepRAG's retrieval setting. We use BM25 top-$k=8$ for HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and MuSiQue, and top-$k=3$ for PopQA. Earlier per-question distractor-pool results are retained only as development and ablation evidence. The final implementation uses a path-aware title policy: title matching is disabled for long original-question direct retrieval and enabled for short decomposed sub-question retrieval.

\section{Stage 4: Synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesize}

The decomposed answer $a^{\mathrm{dec}}$ is produced by a synthesis pass over the original question and all $(s_i,a_i)$ pairs. The prompt encodes three answer-shaping rules derived from error analysis:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Comparison questions} return the matching entity, not the compared value.
    \item \textbf{yes-or-no questions} return ``Yes'' or ``No'' based on agreement between sub-answers.
    \item \textbf{Terse-span rule} strips unnecessary modifiers and keeps answer spans short for EM compatibility.
\end{enumerate}

The implementation also includes a value-to-entity guard for comparison failures: if synthesis emits a value such as a year but the question asks which entity satisfies a comparison, the system maps the value back to the owning entity when this mapping is unambiguous.

\section{Stage 5: Training-Free Verifier v2}
\label{sec:verifier}

The verifier chooses between the direct candidate and the decomposed candidate. It is intentionally conservative: decomposition remains the default because it improves multi-hop retrieval, and the verifier switches to the direct answer only when the direct path is confident and the decomposition path shows signs of instability. The final implementation additionally applies a conservative type-aware fallback that corrects a small number of answer-type mismatches without rerunning generation or training a router.

\paragraph{Signals.} The verifier uses four query-level signals:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct-answer entropy} $\sigma^{\mathrm{dir}}$: low entropy suggests the direct answer may be reliable.
    \item \textbf{Decomposition post-entropy} $\sigma^{\mathrm{dec}}$: high entropy after decomposition and synthesis suggests the decomposed answer is unstable.
    \item \textbf{Structural validity}: unresolved placeholders, empty sub-answers, or malformed sub-questions indicate decomposition failure.
    \item \textbf{Candidate agreement}: normalised string agreement between $a^{\mathrm{dir}}$ and $a^{\mathrm{dec}}$ is treated as mutual support.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Rule.} The verifier accepts agreement immediately; otherwise it chooses direct when the direct answer is confident and decomposition is unstable, and chooses decomposition in the remaining cases. The deployed thresholds are \texttt{direct\_entropy\_tau=0.4} and \texttt{decomp\_post\_entropy\_tau=0.8}. The type-aware fallback is a deterministic refinement: it only changes the final route when answer type, direct entropy, decomposition post-entropy, and entity-fragment signals consistently indicate that the direct answer should be preferred. No dataset name, gold answer, trained classifier, or evidence-label supervision is used.

\paragraph{Evidence support.} We also evaluated evidence-aware variants that score whether the final answer or chain answers are lexically supported by retrieved passages. These variants are not part of the final method: they slightly improve HotpotQA but consistently hurt 2Wiki and MuSiQue, showing that lexical support is not a reliable global routing signal for compositional questions.

\section{Training Protocol}
\label{sec:training}

Only the decomposer requires training. The answerer, synthesizer, and verifier are training-free at the routing stage.

\subsection{Backbone and LoRA Configuration}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone:} Llama-3-8B-Instruct.
    \item \textbf{Adapter type:} LoRA~\cite{lora} on all linear modules.
    \item \textbf{Rank, Alpha, and Dropout:} $r=16$, $\alpha=32$, dropout $=0.05$.
    \item \textbf{Precision:} bf16.
    \item \textbf{Context length:} 1024 tokens.
\end{itemize}

The PopQA component teaches the decomposer that a one-step plan is valid. This improves single-hop robustness, but Chapter~\ref{ch:exp} shows that it also creates negative transfer on some multi-hop datasets. The verifier is introduced precisely because no single hard decomposition policy is optimal across all query types.

\subsection{Optimisation}

LoRA is trained with LLaMA-Factory~\cite{llamafactory}: effective batch size 16, learning rate $1\times10^{-4}$ with cosine decay and 10\% warmup, 3 epochs, and approximately 38 minutes on one A6000 GPU.

\section{Inference Modes}
\label{sec:modes}

Two adapter modes were evaluated during development:

\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{Mode B.}] LoRA is enabled for decomposition and synthesis but disabled for sub-question answering.
    \item[\textbf{Mode C.}] LoRA is enabled for decomposition and sub-question answering. This is the final default because it outperformed Mode B in pilot experiments.
\end{description}

For final synthesis, the adapter is disabled (\textbf{synthOFF}) because the base instruction model produces shorter and more stable final spans.

\section{Implementation Summary}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:seek} summarises the final inference loop. Code lives in the \texttt{seek} module used by the main methodology code.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{SEEK v2 inference for one question $q$}
\label{alg:seek}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require question $q$, corpus $\mathcal{P}$, entropy threshold $\tau$, top-$k$
\State $(a^{\mathrm{dir}}, \sigma^{\mathrm{dir}}) \gets \textsc{DirectAnswer}(q, r(q,\mathcal{P},k))$
\State $\{s_1,\ldots,s_n\}\gets \mathcal{D}_\theta(q)$
\State $A\gets\emptyset$
\For{$i=1$ to $n$}
    \State $s_i\gets\textsc{Resolve}(s_i,A)$
    \State $(\tilde{a}_i,\sigma_i)\gets\mathcal{A}_\theta(s_i,\emptyset)$
    \If{$\sigma_i\geq\tau$}
        \State $c_i\gets r(s_i,\mathcal{P},k) \cup r(q,\mathcal{P},k)$
        \State $a_i\gets\mathcal{A}_\theta(s_i,c_i)$
    \Else
        \State $a_i\gets\tilde{a}_i$
    \EndIf
    \State $A[i]\gets a_i$
\EndFor
\State $(a^{\mathrm{dec}},\sigma^{\mathrm{dec}},z^{\mathrm{dec}})\gets\textsc{Synthesize}(q,\{(s_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{n})$
\State $\hat{a}\gets\textsc{Verifier}(a^{\mathrm{dir}},a^{\mathrm{dec}},\sigma^{\mathrm{dir}},\sigma^{\mathrm{dec}},z^{\mathrm{dec}})$
\State \Return $\hat{a}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
