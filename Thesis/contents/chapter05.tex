\chapter{Results and Analysis}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter builds on the main comparison reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:exp} and analyses why SEEK works and where it fails. It examines retrieval quality, confidence-based ablations, the complementary direct and decomposition paths, final-trace failure modes, and the limits of Tail Leakage as a confidence signal.

\section{Retrieval Analysis}
\label{sec:retrieval-analysis}

This section asks whether decomposition improves retrieval itself, and how much of the improvement depends on the trained decomposer rather than on decomposition as such.

\subsection{Gold and Base Decomposition on MuSiQue}
\label{sec:gold-decomposition-recall}

To separate the value of decomposition from the value of the trained decomposer, we run a focused MuSiQue retrieval-recall analysis using the first 1{,}000 development questions. This slice contains only 2-hop questions, which makes the official MuSiQue gold decomposition directly comparable with SEEK's predicted two-step chain. Table~\ref{tab:musique-gold-recall} reports answer-containing Hit@5 under the same full-corpus Wikipedia and path-aware retrieval setting as Table~\ref{tab:answer-recall}.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \caption[MuSiQue Gold and Base Decomposition Recall]{MuSiQue answer-containing Hit@5 for original, SEEK, gold, and untuned-base decompositions.}
    \label{tab:musique-gold-recall}
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{ l >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{6.5cm} c }
        \toprule
        Arm & Interpretation & Hit@5 \\
        \midrule
        Original question & No decomposition & 19.0 \\
        SEEK & SEEK decomposition with predicted intermediate answer & 38.8 \\
        Gold & Official MuSiQue decomposition with gold intermediate answer & \textbf{55.8} \\
        Gold raw & Official decomposition without replacing \texttt{\#1} & 19.8 \\
        SEEK + gold bridge & SEEK sub-question text with gold intermediate answer & 54.1 \\
        Untuned base + gold bridge & Llama-3-8B-Instruct without LoRA, using gold intermediate answer & 52.9 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

SEEK substantially improves over original-question retrieval but remains below the oracle gold chain. The Gold raw condition falls back near the original-query result, showing that decomposition helps only when the bridge entity is resolved and substituted into the later query. Conditional analysis gives the same conclusion: SEEK approaches gold retrieval when the first-hop answer is correct and falls behind when it is wrong. The remaining gap is therefore driven primarily by bridge-answer drift rather than poor decomposition wording.

The untuned base model also clarifies why the decomposer is trained. Its query text can retrieve well under oracle bridge substitution, but it frequently violates the required step structure or output format. LoRA training therefore provides step-count control, valid placeholders, and compact BM25-friendly queries rather than merely improving wording.

\section{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

This section tests whether retrieval remains necessary after decomposition, how the step-gate threshold affects retrieval and accuracy, and whether title handling should differ between the direct and decomposition paths.

\section{Tail Leakage as a Confidence Signal}
\label{sec:tail-leakage-analysis}

To test whether Tail Leakage adds information beyond established confidence baselines, we group the evaluated signals into six conceptually distinct families and report one representative from each family. Maximum token entropy measures the overall spread of the next-token distribution, while sequence negative log-likelihood measures the probability assigned to the generated answer. The TARG margin uses the separation between the two leading logits~\cite{targ}; sampled-answer disagreement measures response consistency, an established black-box confidence cue~\cite{maqa,semanticuncertainty}; and P(True) asks the model to assess its own proposed answer~\cite{kadavath2022}. Tail Leakage instead measures how much probability escapes a small candidate set, as defined in Section~\ref{sec:probe}. These families test different hypotheses about uncertainty rather than arbitrary transformations of one score.

Table~\ref{tab:signal-battery} reports AUROC for predicting a wrong direct answer on the full trace of each dataset. All scores are oriented so that larger values mean greater uncertainty. Appendix~\ref{app:confidence-signals} gives concise definitions of the reported signals. Tail Leakage obtains the strongest macro result among the representatives. The comparison is diagnostic: small differences within these traces should not be interpreted as a universal ranking across models or datasets.

Most are clean parametric hallucinations with essentially no leakage, while others are alias or answer-boundary near-misses. A confidence threshold cannot identify a confidently produced hallucination when the token distribution itself shows no warning. Appendix~\ref{app:confidence-diagnostics} reports the detailed counts and error categories.

Both-wrong examples generally have higher Tail Leakage, but the overlapping distributions prevent reliable per-question error detection (Appendix~\ref{app:confidence-diagnostics}).
