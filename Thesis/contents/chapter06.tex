\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis introduced \textbf{SEER}, a sub-question-level adaptive retrieval-augmented generation framework that combines a lightweight LoRA decomposer with a training-free entropy gate. The principal contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{KG-triple decomposition for adaptive RAG.} We propose representing each sub-question as an \texttt{entity >> relation} fragment with \texttt{\#N} placeholders for cross-step dependencies. This format is compact, consistent across datasets, and semantically aligned with the entropy probe that follows --- the probed answer span is exactly the object entity of the triple.
    \item \textbf{Per-sub-question entropy gate with a unified threshold.} We show that maximum per-token entropy over the tentative answer span is a reliable confidence signal, and that a single dataset-agnostic threshold $\tau = 0.3$ saves 5--11\% of retrieval calls without sacrificing accuracy. The threshold sweep characterises the plateau--cliff geometry of the entropy distribution and identifies the safe operating region.
    \item \textbf{A single unified model competitive at an order-of-magnitude lower training cost.} Using only a short LoRA SFT pass on the three-source (12K) corpus plus a hand-designed synthesis prompt, one SEER model decisively wins MuSiQue ($+23.7$ EM over published DeepRAG) while remaining competitive on single-hop PopQA ($39.8$ EM, within $2.7$ of specialised DeepRAG; $48.3$ F1, above it) --- with no per-dataset routing. No DPO, no tree search, no preference pairs are required.
    \item \textbf{An interpretable gating signal.} Unlike learned policies, the entropy threshold is a single scalar a practitioner can inspect, tune per deployment, and monitor in production. The threshold-sweep curve also serves as a built-in calibration tool for new datasets.
    \item \textbf{A diagnosed structural limitation.} We identify and characterise the over-decomposition failure mode on single-hop benchmarks, show via ablation that naive single-hop / multi-hop supervision mixing causes negative transfer, and articulate concrete paths to a unified solution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

The principal limitations of the present SEER are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Residual single-hop gap and its multi-hop cost.} On PopQA the unified (12K) model reaches $39.8$ EM, trailing specialised DeepRAG by only $2.7$ EM (and slightly leading it in F1, $48.3$ vs $47.8$), but still short of a plain single-RAG baseline. Including the single-hop training signal that closes most of this gap costs $3$--$7$ EM on 2Wiki / HotpotQA --- the negative-transfer trade-off (Section~\ref{sec:popqa-limit}). The entropy gate is not at fault; the open problem is achieving both regimes in one model without interference.
    \item \textbf{Deep-chain degradation.} On 4-hop MuSiQue subsets, EM drops to 9.5--14.1\%. The decomposer's count-match drops below 50\% at four hops, and errors propagate through the chain.
    \item \textbf{Sequential dependency on first hops.} Wrong sub-answers at early steps poison everything downstream via \texttt{\#N} substitution. SEER has no re-ranking or self-consistency mechanism today.
    \item \textbf{Entropy alone is insufficient at the difficult middle.} The threshold sweep shows that entropy cleanly separates the easy tail but overlaps on harder steps. A second orthogonal signal is needed to push the gate's Pareto frontier further.
    \item \textbf{Greedy decoding only.} All reported numbers are single-seed greedy decoding. Variance bands would strengthen small EM differences but are not reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}

Five directions follow directly from the limitations and discussion:

\subsection{Length-Conditioned Decomposer}

The cleanest remedy to over-decomposition is to make the decomposer conditional on a target depth. Concretely: prepend a control token \texttt{[K=1]}, \texttt{[K=2]}, \ldots, \texttt{[K=4]} during training, and supply the token at inference based on (i) an upstream complexity classifier (Adaptive-RAG-style) or (ii) iterative deepening with a confidence stopping rule. This would let a single LoRA serve both single-hop and multi-hop regimes without the negative transfer observed in Table~\ref{tab:train-data-ablation}.

\subsection{Hybrid Confidence Signals}

The plateau--cliff--decay shape of the threshold curve (Section~\ref{sec:gate-design}) indicates that max-entropy alone cannot disambiguate the difficult middle. Three complementary signals are worth combining:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Self-consistency variance.} Sample $N$ generations and measure inter-sample disagreement; high variance implies low confidence regardless of per-step entropy.
    \item \textbf{Top-1 / Top-2 logit margin.} Captures cases where the model is committed to one wrong token.
    \item \textbf{Hidden-state variance.} As proposed by SeaKR~\cite{seakr}, the residual stream often carries factual uncertainty not visible at the output layer.
\end{itemize}
A learned linear combination of these signals (with $\sim$100 calibration labels per dataset) is a low-cost intervention that should push the cliff further right.

\subsection{Multi-Sample Decomposition with Re-Selection}

Sampling $N=5$ decompositions and selecting the one with the highest end-to-end synthesised-answer confidence (or self-consistency across samples) is a natural complement to entropy gating. It directly addresses the sub-answer propagation failure mode (Section~\ref{sec:em-f1}) by allowing the pipeline to recover from a wrong first hop.

\subsection{Span-Trimming Calibration}

The residual 1--3 EM gap on multi-hop datasets is largely verbosity. A small EM-shaped reward fine-tune (or alternatively a Chain-of-Calibration-style DPO pass on terse vs verbose pairs) would close this gap. This would amount to importing DeepRAG's Stage II selectively, applied only to the synthesis pass --- much cheaper than full DPO.

\subsection{Beyond English Wikipedia}

All experiments use English Wikipedia-derived benchmarks. Extending SEER to multilingual or domain-specific corpora (legal, biomedical, code) would test the cross-domain generality of KG-triple decomposition and entropy gating. The training-free gate is particularly attractive in low-resource settings where DPO preference data is unavailable.

\section{Closing Remarks}

Adaptive retrieval-augmented generation has rapidly converged toward sub-question-level decision making. The two leading designs --- SEER (this work) and DeepRAG --- represent opposite ends of the training-cost spectrum, and the present results suggest that the cheaper, more interpretable end can serve both single- and multi-hop queries with a single unified model. The remaining EM gap on 2Wiki / HotpotQA is real but structurally addressable; the future-work proposals above describe concrete paths forward.

More broadly, we view this thesis as evidence that a well-chosen \emph{representational format} (the KG-triple decomposition) combined with an \emph{interpretable scalar gate} (max-entropy with threshold $\tau$) can substitute for substantial training machinery. As retrieval-augmented systems become embedded in production assistants, that combination of low cost, transparency, and competitive accuracy is likely to be increasingly valuable.
